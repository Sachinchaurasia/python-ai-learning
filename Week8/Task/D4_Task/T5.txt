🧠 TASK 5 — Why can embeddings be useful for semantic search?

Embeddings are useful for semantic search because they can represent the meaning and relationships of text as numerical vectors.

🔹 Traditional keyword search

Suppose you search:

"How to fix a laptop keyboard?"

A simple keyword search mainly looks for matching words such as:

laptop
keyboard
fix

It may struggle if a document says:

"Troubleshooting non-working keys on a notebook computer."

The words are different, even though the meaning is very similar.

🔹 Semantic search with embeddings

Embeddings convert text into vectors:

Query:
"How to fix a laptop keyboard?"
        ↓
   Embedding
        ↓
[0.21, -0.45, 0.78, ...]

A document can also be converted:

"Troubleshooting non-working keys on a notebook computer."
        ↓
   Embedding
        ↓
[0.20, -0.43, 0.76, ...]

If the vectors are sufficiently similar, the search system can recognize that the query and document have related meanings, even though they don't use exactly the same words.

🔥 Simple example

You search:

"How can I learn Java?"

A semantic search system might find:

"Java programming courses for beginners."

Even though the exact words aren't identical, the meaning is closely related.

🧠 Basic process
User Query
    ↓
Embedding
    ↓
Compare with document embeddings
    ↓
Find similar vectors
    ↓
Return relevant documents

A common technique for comparing vectors is cosine similarity.

⭐ Key idea

Embeddings are useful for semantic search because similar meanings can be represented by similar vectors, allowing the system to find relevant information even when the exact keywords don't match.

This is one of the most important real-world applications of embeddings and will become very useful when you build RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) systems later in your AI Engineer roadmap.
